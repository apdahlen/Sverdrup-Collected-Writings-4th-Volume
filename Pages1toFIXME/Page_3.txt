STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards:

* "Ordning" – not merely "arrangement" but an institutional order or administrative settlement.
* "Forstanderinden" – female superintendent/matron with spiritual and administrative authority.
* "Søstre" – diaconal sisters; not generic women.
* "Sagen" – the diaconal cause/work as a vocation, not an abstract issue.
* "Gjerningen" – vocation/calling, with strong religious connotation.
* "Livskald" – life-calling from God, not career choice.
* "Prøvelser" – trials in a providential sense.
* "Station" – outpost/field of service, not a railway sense.
* "Bestyrelse" – governing board with ecclesial authority.

Syntactic Hazards:

* Long, cumulative sentences with concessive clauses.
* Chronological narration interwoven with evaluative commentary.
* Passive constructions conveying providential movement.
* Sermonic pacing even in historical prose.

Theological Hazards:

* Divine calling ("fra Herren").
* Providence in trials and crises.
* Faithfulness versus temptation to abandon the work.
* Ecclesial responsibility and perseverance.

Voice Hazards:

* Calm, sober confidence under trial.
* Avoid modern managerial tone.
* Preserve earnestness without drama.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* "Det gik fremover jevnt omend langsomt" – steady providential progress, not efficiency.
* "Begyndte at lysne til bedre Tider" – metaphor of dawning hope.
* "give op" – spiritual discouragement, not mere resignation.
* "gaa Sagen" – for the work to continue livingly.

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Resumption under new order.
* Gradual recovery of interest.
* External ecclesial developments aiding awareness.
* Emergence of vocation among women.
* Sudden crisis amid hope.
* Severe loss of leadership and personnel.
* Board’s refusal to abandon the work.
* Faith-driven investment despite weakness.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* Calling from the Lord (cf. Romans 12; Galatians 1).
* Trials preceding growth (cf. James 1:2–4).
* Light after darkness imagery.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Maintain sober gravity.
* Do not sentimentalize perseverance.
* Let resolve appear as duty-bound faithfulness.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* Preserve necessity and resolve in decisions taken despite risk.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* "Sagen" → "the cause" consistently.
* "Gjerningen" → "the work" as vocation.
* "Livskald" → "life-calling".

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* Light/darkness metaphors retained.
* Progress imagery preserved.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* Hope → crisis → steadfast resolve.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Preserve long sentences where reasoning accumulates.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION (VOICE-PRESERVING)

Under this new order the work began again. And despite the difficulties it moved forward steadily, though slowly. When the first year came to its end in September 1890, there were, besides the superintendent, seven sisters in the Home, and the cause began to regain something of the interest it had lost. The formation of the United Church in 1890 contributed in a general way to greater ecclesiastical interest, and the diaconal cause became known in wider circles through the fact that it was set forth through the organ of the United Church, the Lutheran Church Paper. A few young women began to consider the work as a life-calling from the Lord.

An anxious crisis.

New trials, however, lay ahead. Just when it seemed to be growing lighter toward better times, the prospects changed once more, and a new crisis entered. In the autumn of 1890 the Deaconess Home received its first station, in that, at the request of the United Church’s Board of Education, it opened an orphanage in Beloit, Iowa, in the buildings which had formerly been used as residence and school facilities for the Augustana Synod’s theological seminary. At the same time the board of the Deaconess Home also began to gather funds for the purchase of land and the building of a suitable house, since the period had expired for which Banker Sunde had promised free use of the house on Hennepin Avenue. Several sisters were also received during the autumn of 1890, so that it seemed as though the work would advance.

But precisely at this point disagreement arose between the board and the superintendent over questions touching the operation and future of the Home. The result was that the superintendent resigned on January 31, 1891, and left the Home in February. Immediately thereafter all the sisters who had been received before the superintendent’s resignation also withdrew, except for the two sisters who were working in the orphanage in Beloit.

In the Deaconess Home in Minneapolis there remained only three newly arrived sisters. A fourth came shortly thereafter. There might have been a temptation to give up; but no such thought arose in the board. A temporary arrangement was made; provision was made for a thorough and earnest instruction of the four sisters; and there was even hope of keeping the cause going, despite the lack of a competent superintendent. So confident was the board that it was precisely at this time that it purchased land and a house in an exceptionally well-situated location, where the Home still remains.
