STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards:

* "Afsnit" – section/division; not merely a paragraph.
* "Diakonisegjerningen" / "Diakonisesagen" – the organized diaconal work, not generic charity.
* "Stuttelser" – setbacks, hindrances, obstructions (not "shortenings").
* "alsidige aandelige Interesser" – many-sided spiritual/intellectual interests; avoid flattening to "broad interests".
* "Udg." – editor’s note; must be preserved as such.
* "historisk Skizze" – historical sketch; modest, provisional tone.

Syntactic Hazards:

* Long editorial sentence with multiple causal clauses.
* Parenthetical framing must be preserved.
* Lists joined by "dels … dels" require balanced rendering.

Theological Hazards:

* Diaconal work is ecclesial and vocational, not merely social.
* Avoid secularizing tone when rendering institutional language.

Voice Hazards:

* Editorial voice is sober, factual, and deferential.
* Avoid modern academic casualness.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* "førte den frem til den Plads" – idiom meaning led it forward to its rightful position.
* "komme frem" – to come to light / be made visible.
* "paa et Sted" – in one place; editorial gathering.

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Identification of subject matter of the section.
* Assertion of Sverdrup’s decisive leadership.
* Justification for selective publication.
* Editorial purpose: historical documentation.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* None explicit; ecclesial-historical prose only.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Maintain calm authority and respect for institutional labor.
* No rhetorical heightening.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* Descriptive, not hortatory; modals are minimal.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* "Diakonisegjerningen" rendered consistently as "the diaconal work".

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* Institutional metaphors of "place" and "leading forward" retained.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* Neutral scholarly tone throughout.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Not sermonic; retain 19th‑century editorial cadence.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION (FINAL)

FIRST SECTION.

[The pieces included in this section deal with the diaconal work. Professor Sverdrup showed not only great interest in the cause of diaconal service; he was also the man who, despite many setbacks, with an energy whose certainty never failed, led it forward to the place it has come to occupy in our ecclesial work. From the relatively large amount he wrote on this matter over the years, only a small selection is published here, partly in order that this side as well of his many‑sided spiritual interests might come to light, and partly also in order to gather in one place some contributions to the early history of the diaconal work in the Norwegian‑American Church. — Ed.]

The Deaconess Home in Minneapolis.

[Source: A manuscript designated by the author as “a historical sketch” and dated 30 November 1897. I have not been able to find it printed anywhere. — Ed.]
