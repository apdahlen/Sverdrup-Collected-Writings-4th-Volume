STEP 1: HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Lexical Hazards:

* “Søstre” refers specifically to consecrated deaconess sisters, not generic sisters.
* “Arbeidet” denotes organized diaconal work, not merely labor.
* “Stationer” refers to mission or service outposts (hospitals, asylums, institutions).
* “Menighedshospital” has ecclesial implications (congregationally founded).
* “Hedningebørn” carries 19th‑century missionary language meaning non‑Christian children.
* “onde Dage” is a culturally and religiously loaded phrase implying days deemed accursed or inauspicious.
* “Vederkvægelse” is an older term meaning refreshment, consolation, spiritual and bodily relief.

Syntactic Hazards:

* Chronological enumeration with embedded subordinate clauses.
* Long sentences joined by semicolons and commas that should not be overly simplified.
* Passive constructions that carry institutional tone.

Theological Hazards:

* References to God’s grace, blessing, and divine providence must retain confessional Lutheran tone.
* The diaconal work is framed as both bodily and spiritual ministry.
* The closing paragraph interprets institutional success theologically, not pragmatically.

Voice Hazards:

* Institutional-historical narration is interwoven with devotional thanksgiving.
* The tone is sober, grateful, and providential, not promotional or sentimental.
* Over‑modernization would erase the ecclesial and confessional register.

STEP 2: IDIOM ANALYSIS

* “ved Guds Naade” — by the grace of God; standard Lutheran confessional idiom.
* “faaet fast Fodfæste” — gained a firm foothold; metaphor of establishment and stability.
* “holde sin Haand over det” — God’s protective providence.
* “sætte det til Velsignelse” — appoint or use something as a blessing; biblical diction.
* “til legemlig og aandelig Vederkvægelse” — echoes biblical language of refreshment and consolation.

STEP 3: ARGUMENT STRUCTURE IDENTIFICATION

* Sequential historical accounting of growth (1892–1897).
* Expansion of personnel and stations demonstrates legitimacy and blessing.
* Appointment of a dedicated pastor addresses a prior deficiency.
* Material growth parallels spiritual and institutional maturation.
* Concluding theological interpretation: success attributed to God’s grace, with prayer for continued blessing.

STEP 4: BIBLICAL ECHO IDENTIFICATION

* “ved Guds Naade” — Ephesians 2:8 resonance.
* “holde sin Haand over det” — biblical imagery of divine protection (e.g., Psalm 139).
* “Velsignelse for Folket og Menigheden” — covenantal language of blessing upon people and congregation.

STEP 5: VOICE INTENSITY PRESERVATION

* Maintain sober gratitude rather than triumphalism.
* Retain explicit attribution of success to God, not human planning alone.
* Preserve earnest hope directed toward divine continuance.

STEP 6: MODAL FORCE PRESERVATION

* Statements of fact remain firm and declarative.
* Expressions of hope (“det er vort Haab”) retain confident expectancy, not mere wishfulness.

STEP 7: CONSISTENT THEOLOGICAL TERM RENDERING

* “Naade” → grace
* “Velsignelse” → blessing
* “Menighed” → congregation
* “Hjemmet” → the Home (Diaconess Home)

STEP 8: METAPHOR INTEGRITY PRESERVATION

* Preserve metaphors of foothold, God’s hand, fruitfulness, and refreshment.
* Avoid explanatory paraphrase that dissolves imagery.

STEP 9: TONE CONTRAST PRESERVATION

* Factual reporting contrasts with devotional conclusion.
* Administrative detail gives way to prayerful hope.

STEP 10: ORAL CADENCE AWARENESS

* Maintain long, cumulative sentences where the original does.
* Preserve semicolon pivots and formal pacing suitable for public reading.

STEP 11: ACADEMIC TRANSLATION (VOICE‑PRESERVING)

In March 1892 the work was taken up at the second station, in that three Sisters were sent to Dr. S. E. Engstad’s Hospital in Grand Forks, North Dakota.

In September 1892 the number of Sisters had increased to fourteen, of whom three labored in the Orphan Home in Beloit and four in the Hospital in Grand Forks.
In September 1893 the number of Sisters was twenty, of whom three were in the Orphan Home in Beloit and three in the Hospital in Grand Forks; and immediately after this time three were sent to the new congregational hospital in Hillsboro, North Dakota.
In September 1894 the number of Sisters was twenty‑seven, of whom twelve labored at four stations outside the Home.
In September 1895 the number of Sisters was thirty‑one, of whom thirteen labored at five stations.
In September 1896 there were thirty‑four Sisters, of whom twenty labored at ten stations.
In October 1897 there were thirty‑eight Sisters, of whom twenty labored at eleven stations; one of the most recently established stations is a children’s asylum at Augsburg, Madagascar, for heathen children who had been born on “evil days” and were therefore cast out.

Not until May 1896 did the Diaconess Home receive its own pastor, when Rev. S. N. Jollefson entered upon this important post after a call from the Home’s Board. A great lack was thereby remedied, and the work has borne fruit through his service in the form of more general interest and more thorough instruction of the Sisters.

In outward respects the Home has also advanced. In 1891, as mentioned above, a house was purchased for 5,500 dollars, and it was immediately enlarged at a cost of 3,500 dollars. In 1892 a building was erected chiefly intended for fever patients, at a cost of 1,800 dollars. In 1897 a house was purchased for sleeping rooms for the Sisters, at a cost of nearly 2,000 dollars.

The diaconal ministry may now, by the grace of God, be said to have gained a firm foothold among the Norwegian Lutherans in America; and it is our hope that, as the beginning has been blessed, so that the work has been a source of bodily and spiritual refreshment for many who suffer, so the Lord will also henceforth hold his hand over it and set it as a blessing for the people and the congregation.
